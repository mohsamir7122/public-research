# Feasibility Protocol: ACL Remnant Preservation and Proprioception

## Status

Dated public feasibility pilot prepared on 2026-08-11. This is not a registered systematic-review protocol and must not be cited as one.

## Review question

Among patients undergoing primary ACL reconstruction, does tibial remnant preservation improve postoperative proprioception compared with standard remnant resection?

## Pilot objectives

1. Generate database-specific candidate queries from a structured PICO.
2. Confirm that comparator and outcome concepts can remain in the PICO while being excluded from the sensitive primary query.
3. Exercise conservative DOI-first deduplication on a small, public metadata seed set.
4. Apply a dated human novelty gate before any protocol registration, screening, or synthesis.

## Information sources and scope

The seed metadata were manually verified against PubMed, PubMed Central, ClinicalTrials.gov, and a publisher landing page. They are not a complete export from a systematic search. Candidate Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, and PubMed queries were generated offline and were not executed in this pilot.

## Eligibility concept for a future review

A future registered review would require comparative human primary studies of primary ACL reconstruction with versus without tibial remnant preservation, with an objectively measured proprioception outcome such as Joint Position Sense or Threshold to Detect Passive Motion. Exact eligibility rules, language limits, dates, study designs, and outcome timing must be frozen only after the question is reframed and novelty is rechecked.

## Stop rule

Stop before registration or screening when a recent directly overlapping systematic review already addresses the same population, intervention, comparator, and outcome without a defensible new update signal or methodological distinction.

## Current decision

The broad question stopped at the novelty gate. Reviews published in 2022, 2024, and 2025 overlap directly, and the 2025 review searched through November 2024 and included proprioception. The 2026 cohort is relevant for surveillance but does not alone establish a need for a duplicate broad review.

## Activities not performed

No comprehensive database search, title or abstract screening, full-text screening, outcome extraction, Risk of Bias assessment, certainty assessment, meta-analysis, clinical recommendation, or manuscript drafting was performed.
